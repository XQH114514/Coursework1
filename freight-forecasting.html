Freight forecasting and workforce scheduling

A data-driven planning project for a logistics sorting center. The work connected thirty-day and hourly freight forecasts with practical decisions about capacity and staffing.

01

Problem

Future handling demand is uneven, while staffing must be arranged before the actual workload is known.

The project addressed two linked questions: how much freight would arrive over the next thirty days, and how many permanent and temporary workers should be scheduled to meet that demand. A useful plan required both aggregate volume and variation between shorter time periods.

02

Method

The workflow moved from data quality to forecast reliability and operational scheduling.

  1. 01Clean missing values and outliers
  2. 02Construct time-period features
  3. 03Forecast total and hourly freight
  4. 04Balance volume across periods
  5. 05Optimize the staffing mix and schedule

Data preparation

Linear interpolation filled missing observations, while abnormal values were identified and handled before modeling.

Feature construction

Time-period features reflected recurring temporal patterns in freight volume and improved the forecasting structure.

Forecasting

A linear regression model was selected to capture the strong temporal relationship in the project data.

Scheduling

Heuristic search explored worker combinations and shift arrangements to reduce cost while matching forecast demand.

04

What it demonstrates

Combining forecasting, optimization, and operational communication.

The project demonstrates end-to-end analytical work: improving data quality, constructing features, selecting a forecast model, translating predictions into staffing constraints, and presenting the result as an actionable plan.
