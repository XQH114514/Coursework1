National innovation and entrepreneurship project

Fuzzy Multi-Attribute Decision Making and Its Applications

模糊多属性决策及其应用

A year-long research project at Nanchang University examining how fuzzy mathematics and outranking methods support decisions when criteria, preferences, and information are uncertain.

01

Overview

Many real decisions involve conflicting criteria that cannot be measured with one exact number.

The project reviewed fuzzy multi-attribute decision-making from its foundations to practical application. It connected fuzzy sets, membership and fuzzy measures with ranking methods such as ELECTRE I, II, and III, then examined how extended fuzzy information can support supplier selection and online-platform evaluation.

02

Method

A structured path from alternatives and criteria to an explainable ranking.

  1. 01Define alternatives and decision criteria
  2. 02Represent uncertain judgments as fuzzy values
  3. 03Estimate or assign criterion weights
  4. 04Build concordance and discordance relations
  5. 05Derive and interpret the final ordering

Fuzzy set foundations

Membership functions, fuzzy measures, fuzzy-set operations, and Hamming or Euclidean distance.

ELECTRE outranking

Pairwise comparison using concordance, discordance, preference thresholds, and an outranking graph.

Interval Pythagorean hesitant fuzzy information

Representing uncertainty and hesitation when participants provide more than one possible membership value.

Entropy and cross-entropy

Using information differences to estimate attribute weights and construct net concordance and discordance indices.

03

Results

Two application cases show how the methods produce a defensible overall ranking.

Supplier selection x2 ranked first

Four international suppliers were compared across cost, quality, service, image, and risk.

Platform evaluation A2 ranked first

Five online shopping platforms were evaluated under five consumer-facing criteria.

Complete ordering A2 > A1 > A4 > A5 > A3

The second case produced a complete preference order rather than only a winning option.

Derived weights 0.2164 / 0.1953 / 0.1811 / 0.2063 / 0.2009

Attribute weights were estimated when they were initially unknown, reducing reliance on subjective scoring.

These are the report's illustrative application results. They demonstrate the decision process and should not be interpreted as universal market rankings.

04

Contribution

The work connects mathematical foundations, method comparison, and applied examples in one coherent research report.

It explains when fuzzy set extensions are needed, how ELECTRE II constructs a preference relation, and how entropy-based information can support weighting and evaluation. The application cases demonstrate how the method can produce an explainable ranking rather than an isolated score.
